VTTReading Vehicle Size and Powertrain from Motion

Sixty seconds of how a car moves — speed, pedals, steering, yaw, the gap to the car ahead — carry a readable signature of what is moving. On car models never seen in training, powertrain (BEV vs ICE) reads at 93.0 drive-level macro-F1; size stays at 67.1, and zero-training ceiling analyses say why.

VTT is a trajectory + CAN dataset at 10 Hz: 9,062 drives, 3,949.8 hours, 1,431 pseudonymous vehicles, 174 car models and 201,390 eligible 60-s windows, each drive labelled with its platform's footprint size class (SMALL / MID / LARGE; cuts 4.43 / 4.88 m²) and powertrain (ICE / HEV / PHEV / BEV). The headline split holds out 41 car models; four sequence architectures, feature baselines, a pre-registered method campaign and an episode-centric multiple-instance model are scored under window-, drive- and vehicle-level protocols.

01 · OVERVIEW

Can you tell what kind of vehicle produced a trace from how it moves?

The question, the release and the headline result, beside what you need at a glance.

From 60-s windows of trajectory and CAN signals — speed, acceleration, steering angle, rate and normalised torque, yaw rate, curvature, lateral acceleration, pedal flags, the automation flag and the lead vehicle's distance, relative speed, acceleration, headway and time to collision — we ask whether a vehicle's size class and powertrain type can be inferred for car models never seen in training. VTT releases 9,062 drives (3,949.8 h) from 1,431 pseudonymous vehicles and 174 car models with model-disjoint and device-disjoint splits and window-, drive- and vehicle-level protocols; benchmarks four sequence architectures (1-D ResNet, bi-GRU, Transformer, bidirectional Mamba-2), hand-crafted statistics and LLM-proposed physical features under those protocols; and then runs a pre-registered method campaign on model-disjoint internal folds — car-model-balanced sampling, auxiliary physical targets, physics-safe augmentation, DAS down-weighting and an episode-centric multiple-instance model.

On the 41 held-out car models, BEV vs ICE reaches 92.6–93.0 drive-level macro-F1 (vehicle level 92.8–93.4); size, three footprint classes, improves from 62.9 to 67.1 at drive level but stays far from 90. Zero-training oracle analyses — a per-car-model majority oracle, a footprint-noise oracle, a curb-mass oracle, evidence sufficiency and the separability of the held-out models — indicate that the size gap is set by the label resolution and the composition of the training fleet rather than by model capacity or the evidence available per drive.

What a window model sees

Only the 20 official channels of 600 consecutive 10 Hz samples — no brand, platform, region or software metadata, which are kept out of the inputs because they carry shortcuts. Here the car is human-driven (no DAS activity in the window or the 10 s before it): top speed 47 km/h, accelerations between −3.2 and +3.3 m/s², the accelerator pressed 85 % of the time and the brake 9 %.

A drive's decision is the mean of its windows' probability vectors followed by arg-max; the running mean along a drive is what the fleet-level tables score at drive level. Vehicle level pools all test drives of a pseudonymous vehicle the same way.

03 · EVIDENCE

What the numbers rest on

Headline split: 41 car models held out, three seeds, drive-level macro-F1 with a 95 % cluster bootstrap over car models. Results are reference points under a fixed protocol, not a leaderboard.

93.0 drive-level macro-F1 · BEV vs ICE · 41 held-out car models

Powertrain reads on car models never seen in training.

1-D ResNet with recipe B5: 93.0 drive-level macro-F1, 95 % CI [87.2, 96.6]; the bi-GRU B5 reaches 92.6 and, at vehicle level, 93.4. The reference recipes already sit at 88.4–91.1; the recipe adds 2–4 points. Bar = drive-level macro-F1, second value = window level.

67.1 drive-level macro-F1 · size · from 62.9

Size improves with the recipe — and stays far from 90.

bi-GRU with recipe S9: 67.1 drive-level macro-F1 (CI [54.4, 74.2]) against 62.9 for the reference bi-GRU; window level stays at about 53.2. The four deep architectures lie within about two points of each other at window level and within three at drive level, so architecture is not the bottleneck; drive-level aggregation adds 7–9 points to the reference models and 14 with the recipe.

92.1 size macro-F1 of a footprint oracle at σ = 0.10 m²

The size gap points to label resolution, not model capacity.

Give an oracle the true footprint plus per-drive noise and cut it at the thresholds: 92.1 at σ = 0.10 m², 86.6 at 0.15, 80.7 at 0.20 — reaching 90 needs an error of about ±0.12 m², roughly 2.5 % of a 4.6 m² footprint. 21 of the 41 held-out car models lie within 0.15 m² of a differently-labelled training model. A per-car-model majority oracle on the reference predictions scores 72.0; two curb-mass cuts fitted on the training drives score 63.3.

63.8–73.8 % size drive accuracy by windows per drive · flat

Evidence per drive is not the bottleneck either.

Drive accuracy by number of windows per drive stays between 63.8 and 73.8 % for size and 85.7 and 93.2 % for BEV vs ICE from 1–2 windows to more than 64. Subsampling m windows on drives with at least 32: size 52.6 → 54.9 macro-F1 from m = 1 to 32 (all windows 56.0); BEV vs ICE 79.7 → 89.3, saturating by 8–16 windows. Accuracy does not grow with the number of acceleration or deceleration episodes either.

9,062 → 41 drives in the release · car models held out for the test

Scope, stated plainly.

The release: 9,062 drives, 3,949.8 nominal hours (3,078.1 moving), 1,431 pseudonymous vehicles on 360 platforms of 38 brands, 174 car models, 201,390 eligible 60-s windows. Hours per size class: SMALL 1,784 (44.7 %), MID 1,124 (28.1 %), LARGE 1,037 (25.9 %); eligible windows per powertrain: ICE 79,566, HEV 23,708, PHEV 10,585, BEV 64,968; platforms sold with several powertrains carry no powertrain label (11.1 % of the drives). In the headline scheme the identity group — same device, same vehicle identity or shared drive names — is the unit, 41 car models form the test split, and the validation split shares 53 of its 56 car models with training, so only the test split is model-disjoint. Chance level is about 33 for size and 50 for BEV vs ICE; 62.6 % of the samples show DAS activity, so part of every signature is the controller's.

Window-level accuracy by distance of the footprint to the nearest cut: 53.0 % within 0.1 m², 59.6 % at 0.1–0.2, 75.4 % at 0.2–0.4, 89.9 % beyond 0.8.

05 · METHOD

Window models, an episode model, and a protocol that cannot peek

Sequence models on 600-sample windows of the 20 official channels; a recipe pre-registered on model-disjoint internal folds; an episode-centric multiple-instance model over driving transients; and an evaluation that holds out whole car models and bootstraps over them.

Inputs and labels

Drive tables are indexed by car-state sample (row k = k-th 10 Hz sample); radar, pose and controller topics are zero-order held causally onto the sample times. Footprint = wheelbase × mean track per platform; the cuts 4.43 / 4.88 m² were fixed from the label inventory alone before any classifier (every size × {BEV, ICE} cell ≥ 100 h, ≥ 3 car models, ≥ 5 identity groups). Pedal magnitudes are missing on many vehicles, so only the pressed flags are inputs.

Reference recipe

Class-balanced sampling with weights (N / K n_k)^0.5 capped at 10, AdamW, warm-up + cosine, label smoothing 0.05, batch 256, at most 25 epochs with early stopping on the validation macro-F1. 1-D ResNet 2.31 M parameters, bi-GRU 0.73 M, Transformer 0.70 M, bidirectional Mamba-2 1.07 M (a pure-PyTorch implementation of the published chunked state-space-duality scan).

LLM-proposed physical features

Six proposer agents, one physics lens each, saw only the channel definitions and a training-only sample of 2,727 windows; verifiers ran and fixed the code, an auditor removed duplicates, speed confounds and cues that come from the automation controller; 34 of 74 proposals were registered before any evaluation split was touched and fed gradient boosting. Lift-off deceleration features rank highest for powertrain; curvature-per-steering gains for size.

Honest limits. Size stays far from 90 on unseen car models, and the ceilings say why: the cuts fall inside dense regions of the fleet (21 of 41 held-out models within 0.15 m² of a differently-labelled training model), LARGE is learned largely from pickups so large unibody cars read MID or SMALL, and a footprint oracle needs about ±0.12 m² of error to reach 90 — a label-resolution and fleet-composition limit, not a capacity one. Also: one logger ecosystem and a region skew (83.8 % of the hours in North America); labels are platform attributes, so a misidentified platform mislabels every drive of that vehicle; platforms sold with several powertrains are excluded from task B; 62.6 % of the samples carry DAS activity, so part of the signature belongs to the controller (human-only protocols are reported for that reason); the validation split shares car models with training, so recipe decisions were taken on model-disjoint internal folds and the final models train for a fixed epoch count; the case drives are hand-picked; the paper is in submission and its numbers may still change.
